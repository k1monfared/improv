## 1. Nice and Nasty
**Players:** Whole class, working to the person beside them
**Setup:** None. Used before the word status is introduced, to dodge resistance.
**Goal:** Experience raising and lowering sensations without theory.
**Rules:** 1. Say something nice to the person beside you. 2. Then say something nasty. 3. Notice the laughter and surprise when the effect lands wrong.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** Some people never really say anything nice and others never say anything really nasty, and they do not realise it. Experience first, discuss implications afterwards.

## 2. Hallo Milling with Eye Contact Rules
**Players:** Whole group
**Setup:** Open space
**Goal:** Feel dominance and submission through eye contact alone.
**Rules:** 1. Mill about saying hallo to each other. The group feels awkward because nobody knows what status to play. 2. Assign half the group to hold every eye contact for a couple of seconds. 3. Assign the other half to make eye contact, break it, then immediately glance back for a moment. 4. Mill again. The group suddenly looks real: some dominant, some submissive.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** Holders report feeling powerful and visibly look it. Breakers-and-glancers feel feeble and look feeble. Students like doing this and are puzzled by the strength of the sensation.

## 3. The Er Experiment
**Players:** Teacher demonstrating, then class experimenting
**Setup:** None
**Goal:** Show how tiny vocal placement changes perceived status.
**Rules:** 1. Speaker inserts a tentative short er at the start of each sentence: perceived helpless and weak, though listeners cannot say what changed. 2. Move the er into the middle of sentences: perceived a little stronger. 3. Make the er long and return it to the start: perceived more important, more confident. 4. Class tries it.
**Variations:** Videotape or mirror work for students who cannot hear themselves.
**Coaching:** Many people unconsciously resist the short er, substituting um or elongating the sound: they cling to self-importance. The short er invites interruption, the long er says do not interrupt me even though I have not thought what to say yet.

## 4. Still Head Speaking
**Players:** Any speaker, class observing
**Setup:** Mirror or videotape useful
**Goal:** Acquire the highest-value authority signal.
**Rules:** 1. Speak while keeping the head absolutely still. 2. Notice how other high-status behaviours follow automatically: complete sentences, held eye contact, smooth movement, occupied space.
**Variations:** Practice in front of a mirror or on tape.
**Coaching:** Some find still-head speaking impossible, and some insist the head is still while jerking it about. Actors needing authority, tragic heroes, officers issuing commands, must learn it. You can waggle your head as the gravedigger, not as Hamlet.

## 5. Status Trick Conversations
**Players:** Pairs
**Setup:** None
**Goal:** Experience status changes through assigned physical tricks.
**Rules:** Hold conversations together while trying out assigned contrasts: 1. One moves very smoothly, the other jerkily. 2. One keeps putting his hands near his face while speaking, the other keeps hands away. 3. One holds toes pointing inward, the other sits back and spreads out.
**Variations:** Any body-behaviour contrast pair.
**Coaching:** Apparently unrelated things strongly influence each other: feet position changes sentence structure, hesitations, smiles, breathiness. If you speak with a still head you do many other high-status things automatically.

## 6. Minimal-Gap Status Scenes
**Players:** Pairs before the class
**Setup:** None
**Goal:** The founding exercise. Reproduce real-life moment-by-moment status adjustment.
**Rules:** 1. Play scenes in which: both lower status, both raise status, one raises while the other lowers, or the status reverses during the scene. 2. Insist the gap be just a little above or below the partner's. 3. Grade transitions smoothly: if a photograph were taken every five seconds the prints should sort themselves into order by status shown.
**Variations:** Reversal scenes emphasised: easy in one jump, valuable graded delicately.
**Coaching:** Minimal gaps force you to really see your partner. Watching someone try to be high and fail is as delightful as watching success.

## 7. The Bystander
**Players:** 3
**Setup:** Restaurant or similar setting
**Goal:** Add texture to two-person status scenes.
**Rules:** 1. Two players play a status scene, for instance quarrelling with the waiter. 2. The third is instructed to try not to get involved. 3. Play.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** The bystander's very subtle manoeuvrings are a delight to watch.

## 8. Status Sequence Runs
**Players:** Pairs through several scenes
**Setup:** None
**Goal:** Build confidence by chaining statuses across scenes.
**Rules:** 1. Breakfast scene: husband plays low, wife high. 2. Office scene: husband plays high to a secretary who plays low. 3. Supper scene: both play low. Continue linking.
**Variations:** Any chain of contrasting configurations.
**Coaching:** Once the status becomes automatic, as in life, complex scenes can be improvised with no preparation. The exercises are crutches so instinctual systems can operate.

## 9. Entering the Wrong Room
**Players:** One student at a time
**Setup:** Real classroom door
**Goal:** Make a status transition physically real instead of acted.
**Rules:** 1. Student leaves the room, then comes in through the real door acting entering the wrong room. 2. Coach rejects phoney solutions: entering head down, walking backwards, advancing two paces, act-seeing the audience, leaving phoney. 3. Ask for what we all do in life: the radar scans the new space for dangers. 4. Once real, set students to predetermine the direction of the status change.
**Variations:** Smiling with top teeth only helps a low entrance, turning your back on exit helps a high one. Videotape for difficult cases.
**Coaching:** You prepare a status for one situation and must alter it when confronted by the unexpected one.

## 10. The Governors Play
**Players:** 1, class watching
**Setup:** Invented at RADA to push students into emotional experience. Register and a pair of glasses as props.
**Goal:** Real emotion through status collapse, repeatable escalation.
**Rules:** 1. Player enters late as a teacher carrying register and glasses, treats the class as his form: All right, quiet there, now then. 2. He puts the glasses on and sees not the class but a meeting of the school Governors. 3. Apologises, dropping in status frantically, struggles to the door. 4. The door sticks, wrestle for about ten seconds, huge status drop. 5. Exit, then optionally re-enter and replay: finding someone in the staff-room and chatting about tea, putting the glasses on again; visiting a psychiatrist; confessing to God.
**Variations:** Any profession and any wrong audience.
**Coaching:** Can turn people into crumbling wrecks in a short time, revelation for pretence-actors. Never push anyone into it, and the class must understand people are allowed to get upset without being called exhibitionistic.

## 11. Status in Gibberish
**Players:** Pairs or groups
**Setup:** After basic status exercises work
**Goal:** Prove that what is said matters less than the status played.
**Rules:** Repeat any status exercise in gibberish, for instance one high meeting one low and reversing status in an imaginary language.
**Variations:** All status exercises take gibberish well.
**Coaching:** Audiences laugh amazingly though nobody knows what is being said. The status reversal alone enthrals.

## 12. Every Status on a Fragment
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Short learned fragment: A is late, B has been waiting. A: Hallo. B: Hallo. A: Been waiting long? B: Ages.
**Goal:** Play any status on fixed words, separating text from transaction.
**Rules:** 1. Both high: A strolls on with all the time in the world, Hallo as if not late at all; B holds eye contact,Hallo with emphasis. 2. Both low: A arrives running; B stands, bends head forward, low-status smile; breathless exchanges, anxiety, weak laugh on Ages. 3. Mixed configurations at will.
**Variations:** Any short fragment serves, office scenes such as Sir and Smith below.
**Coaching:** The implication of the words is that B lowers A, but any status can be played on them.

## 13. Sir and Smith
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Sir's office. Dialogue skeleton: Sir: Come in. Sit down Smith. You know why I sent for you? Smith: No, Sir. (newspaper pushed across desk) Smith: I was hoping you wouldn't see that. Sir: We can't employ anyone with a criminal record. Smith: Won't you reconsider? Sir: Good-bye, Smith. Smith: I never wanted your bloody job anyway.
**Goal:** Status against resistance, and status toward space.
**Rules:** 1. Play the scene with Smith high: Won't you reconsider meets enormous resistance, gripping. 2. Play Sir's Good-bye, Smith low status. 3. Complication: Smith plays low status to the SPACE even when playing high to Sir, otherwise it looks like his office. 4. Sir plays high status to the space even when playing low to Smith, otherwise he looks like an intruder. Coach: Move about. Answer the telephone. Walk over to the window.
**Variations:** Any authority-interview skeleton.
**Coaching:** Status is played to anything, objects as well as people. A king may play low to a subject, but not to his palace.

## 14. Status to Objects
**Players:** 1
**Setup:** Bare stage with a bench
**Goal:** Solo status practice, environment as partner.
**Rules:** 1. Actor waits onstage. Coach asks what status he is playing; he says he has not started. 2. Play low status to the bench: he glances around as if in a park he suspects is private. 3. Play low status to the pigeon: mimes feeding it, convincingly now. 4. More pigeons arrive, one lands on his arm, another shits on him; wipes the mess off surreptitiously.
**Variations:** Any environment, high or low to any object.
**Coaching:** He does not need another actor to play status scenes with, anything in the environment will do.

## 15. Non-Defence, Played Low
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Conflict scene: siblings sharing a flat, B enters asking about his letters, finds A opens them.
**Goal:** The best defence is none: total acceptance deflects aggression.
**Rules:** 1. First run naturally: denials, escalation, near violence, stop the scene. 2. Restart: A admits everything while playing low status. B: Did you open my letter? A: Yes. B pauses: Yes? A: Yes. B: Well what did you do it for? A: I wanted to see what was inside. 3. B encouraged to be angry and close in; A keeps agreeing: You're right to be angry. I'm a shit. I always do it. 4. If shaken, A cries, nods, says You're right, you're right. 5. End: Well, don't open them again. A: I poke my nose into everything.
**Variations:** Any interrogation frame.
**Coaching:** The more A accepts B's dominance the more powerfully B is deflected, like the wolf exposing neck and underbelly. Players leap about with joy afterwards, controlling the attacker like a puppet.

## 16. Non-Defence, Played High
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Same letters scene, taught after low version is mastered
**Goal:** Maintain high status while making no verbal defence.
**Rules:** 1. Same dialogue but calmly, sipping coffee as if no attack were being made: Yes... I open your letters. Dismissive. 2. Catch concealed low-status leakage: arm climbing to the back of the chair as if to flee, foot starting to tap. 3. Weight the situation heavily in A's favour: set it in A's house, B a guest who arrived late the night before, tasked with asking for the daughter's hand in marriage. Sample: You must be John... Cynthia tells me you want to marry her... Oh, by the way, a letter came for you this morning. It's been opened. I open everyone's letters... It's from your mother. Some of it I thought most unsuitable. You'll see I crossed some paragraphs out.
**Variations:** Any host-guest asymmetry.
**Coaching:** At first hardly anyone can carry it off, they pretend high but are crumbling. Reinforce the position until pure verbal non-defence sustains it.

## 17. Insult Lists and Shop Scenes
**Players:** Whole class divided in two halves, then pairs
**Setup:** Each half writes a list of insulting names: fool, slut, pig, arsehole, jerk, meatface, dumbhead, flatfoot, pigeyes, bugeyes, buckteeth, cowflop, monkeyface, swine, rathead. Only half the class knows who suggested each, and each suggestion carries the group's stamp of approval.
**Goal:** Liberate the class so status work gets easier. The insult is the verbal custard pie.
**Rules:** 1. Set aside the lists. First play boringly literal shop scenes: Can I help you? I'd like a pair of shoes. Would these do? Another colour. Coach insists they buy something and sell something, no attempts to be interesting. 2. Hand out the lists: add ONE insult to the end of each sentence. Can I help you, fool? Yes, bugeyes! Delightful but boring. 3. Explain we want to see someone INSULTED: repeat every insult in disbelief and outrage. Why, I... Flat-footed pig yourself! Flat-footed pig! You call me a flat-footed pig, you... you... Arsehole! Buy something! I want a hat, buckteeth! Buckteeth! Try this for size, jerk!
**Variations:** Rotate the lists so terrible things get said to everybody; later give one actor a list and let the other invent his own; set the scenes among people, master-servant teams meeting; gibberish version repeating the last sound of each insult sentence.
**Coaching:** Insults must remain ornamentation, never become the scene itself. Bound people relax into physical freedom, gestures flow. Deeply releasing: status lowering so drastic and so pleasurable that ordinary status scenes hold fewer terrors.

## 18. Group Insult Approach
**Players:** Two groups
**Setup:** Groups at opposite sides of the room
**Goal:** Enormous energy release, collective permission.
**Rules:** 1. Groups slowly approach each other, individuals running forward to hurl insults then dragged back by friends. 2. Target is the opposing GROUP, never individual members. 3. Teacher ensures every insult is received. 4. Usually ends with both groups face to face screaming, everyone having a very good time.
**Variations:** Repeat in mime. Makes a good aside exercise: whoever is insulted repeats the word to the audience.
**Coaching:** Useful even in schools with censored lists, or entirely in gibberish.

## 19. Space Streaming Demo
**Players:** 2 volunteers, class watching
**Setup:** Face to face about a foot apart
**Goal:** Make territorial space tangible.
**Rules:** 1. Volunteers stand facing, close. Note the strong desire to change body position. 2. If they hold still, love or hate feelings arise as space streams into each other. 3. They will modify positions until space flows relatively unhindered, or step back to weaken the force.
**Variations:** Kneeling, bowing, prostrating demonstrated as ritualised low-status ways of shutting space off. Sergeant-major humiliation: recruit to attention, scream from an inch away.
**Coaching:** High-status players let their space flow into others, low-status players avoid letting it flow out.

## 20. Reading Posture Direction
**Players:** 1 demonstrator, class guessing
**Setup:** Neutral symmetrical seat on a bench
**Goal:** Show that trivial adjustments redirect all the space.
**Rules:** 1. Sit neutrally, then cross left leg over right: space visibly flows to the right, the leg an aerofoil. 2. Rest the right arm along the bench back: space flows more strongly that way. 3. Turn the head right: practically all the space flows right. Anyone sitting neutrally in the beam reads lower status. 4. Cross left wrist over right: direction follows the top hand.
**Variations:** Any seated posture change.
**Coaching:** The class is amazed every time: the difference seems trivial yet the effect is strong.

## 21. Fear Crouch and Cherub Posture
**Players:** Demonstrated, then tried by all
**Setup:** Open space
**Goal:** Two opposite full-body status signatures.
**Rules:** 1. Fear crouch: shoulders lift to protect the jugular, body curls forward to protect the underbelly. 2. Cherub posture: open all planes, head turns and tilts offering the neck, shoulders counter-turn to expose the chest, spine arches back and twists so pelvis opposes shoulders, exposing the underbelly. Sign of vulnerability and tenderness, powerful on onlookers.
**Variations:** Challenge drills: challenge a low-status player and watch the slide toward crouch; high-status players adopt cherub variants and, attacked, straighten but never crouch.
**Coaching:** High-status people often use versions of the cherub posture. Learners find both shapes startling to hold.

## 22. Street Approach Distances
**Players:** Pairs, working in public streets
**Setup:** Outdoor homework-style exercise
**Goal:** Calibrate real social distances against strangers.
**Rules:** 1. Leaflet variant: hand out leaflets. You must establish you are giving leaflets and present one at the right moment, or passers-by ignore you or show alarm. 2. Recognition variant: one student recognises strangers, Hi! How are you? How's the family? Same old job then? The partner acts bored, Sorry? Come on, we'll be late, general impatience. 3. Debrief timing: nervous players mistime the initial approach and greet people sweeping past as if they did not exist.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** Great advantage: you cannot dismiss real people's reactions as untruthful.

## 23. Statue Study and Relocation
**Players:** 2 statues, rest study
**Setup:** Arrange two students in some spatial relationship
**Goal:** Expose unconscious mutual adjustment.
**Rules:** 1. Others study the pair; many will not understand what holds the pose. 2. Lift the two statues, chairs and all, and place them on opposite sides of each other. 3. Compare: the space which seemed so natural now looks weird.
**Variations:** Freeze-tag style variants of the coach's invention.
**Coaching:** Everyone sees how carefully the pair adjusted their movements to fit each other.

## 24. Joining and Leaving Homework
**Players:** Solo observers
**Setup:** Cafes, bus stops, any real groups
**Goal:** Train perception of the kinetic adjustments around membership.
**Rules:** 1. Watch groups of people. 2. Notice how everyone's attitude shifts when anyone joins or leaves. 3. Watch two people together, wait for one to leave, and watch the remaining person rearrange posture to express relationship to the people around him.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** Assigned as homework in the original teaching.

## 25. Master-Servant Foundation
**Players:** 2 or more
**Setup:** Any service setting
**Goal:** The defining rule of master-servant play.
**Rules:** 1. Johnstone's law: both parties act as if ALL the space belonged to the master. 2. Servant's preferred position: the edge of the master's parabola of space. Crossing in front, shrink; behind the master, may be close and visibly higher; near a door so he can be dismissed instantly. 3. Mime dressing and undressing each other as master and servant, the clearest feedback on correct space. 4. Build scene structures: nice master with nasty servant, nasty master with nice servant, then the two households interrelating toward a duel, Commedia style, sometimes duelling piggyback.
**Variations:** Henpecked husbands and dominant wives, chauffeurs, any asymmetric pair.
**Coaching:** Trained servants can throttle the master while remaining visibly the servant. The audience delights in the contrast between status played between characters and status played to the space.

## 26. Keeping the Servant on the Hop
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Service scene
**Goal:** Teach acceptance plus deflection, the engine of compliant wit.
**Rules:** 1. Master objects to everything the servant is, says or does. 2. Servant ACCEPTS the master's statement then deflects it. Why are you wearing that ridiculous uniform? It's my birthday, Sir. Correct. 3. Wrong answers reject the premise or blame the master: I'm not wearing a uniform, Sir, or You told me to, Sir. The master always boggles a moment at a correct reply, mind readjusting. 4. Coffee example: Where's the sugar? It's in, Sir. Correct: accepted that the master takes sugar and that none is visible. What about your diet, Sir? would be feeble.
**Variations:** Feeds the Making Faces master games in Impro for Storytellers.
**Coaching:** Recognition test: a correct reply makes the master boggle.

## 27. Servant in Trouble
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Service scene
**Goal:** Generate scene content from self-incrimination.
**Rules:** 1. Servant volunteers disasters: Why are you wearing that uniform, Smith? I burned the other one, Sir. Where's the sugar? I've eaten the last lump, Sir. 2. Time-collapse variant: Good morning, Jenkins. I'm afraid it's not morning, Sir. I forgot to wake you. It's four o'clock in the afternoon. Don't you know what day it is? Your coronation, Sir. 3. Over-confession Jazzi, from Molire: Ah Perkins, I have a bone to pick with you! Not the rhubarb patch, Sir. ... the servant confesses goat, dog, poisoning, and the daughter, always one crime ahead of the accusation.
**Variations:** Any household or palace.
**Coaching:** Audiences laugh a lot at the over-confession pattern whatever the literary value.

## 28. Solo Master-Servant
**Players:** 1
**Setup:** Soft hat
**Goal:** Accelerate both roles in one player.
**Rules:** 1. Wearing the hat he is the master. 2. Removes it and leaps into the position of the servant he was imagining. 3. The moment he cannot think what to say, change roles. 4. He may throttle himself, beat himself up, praise himself.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** Blocks far less than paired play, and the mind splits itself readily, as in Perls top-dog underdog work.

## 29. Mouth and Body Split
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** One actor speaks both parts, the other mouths the words supposed to be his
**Goal:** Sustain long master-servant scenes through shared control.
**Rules:** 1. Voice actor runs both voices. 2. Mouthing actor starts passive; prod him to develop action: picking up a chair and threatening the master forces the master's next line. 3. Escalate: master beats the servant and supplies all screams and pleas himself.
**Variations:** Precursor of Lip-Sync in Impro for Storytellers.
**Coaching:** Sounds difficult, actually easier to sustain than normal two-voice scenes.

## 30. Numbered Pecking Orders
**Players:** 3 or more, numbered by rank
**Setup:** Numbers assigned instantly, any setting
**Goal:** Assemble hierarchies on the spur of the moment and cartoon real organisational life.
**Rules:** 1. Orders and blame pass one way along the hierarchy, excuses and problems the other way. 2. Interact mainly with adjacent rank. 3. Balloon variant: give each actor a long balloon. If One hits Two, Two apologises and hits Three, down the line; the bottom rank ducks, cries, bites his lip or dies. 4. Faces: each person may make faces at anyone ABOVE him without getting caught; if One sees Three make a face at Two he informs Two.
**Variations:** Crowd scenes: numbering extras fixes the phoney spacing of most crowd work.
**Coaching:** Simple rules produce amazing permutations. Number One suppresses everything that irritates him; Number Four makes excuses, evades responsibility, invents problems to pass up the line.

## 31. Taking the Hat
**Players:** 4 numbered players
**Setup:** Soft trilby hats
**Goal:** Clown pecking-order chaos with total commitment.
**Rules:** 1. Number One takes Number Two's hat and throws it at his feet. Two reacts with horror and embarrassment and shrieks for Number Three to pick it up and replace it. Two then takes Three's hat, and so on; Four must put his own hat on. 2. Then tell One he can take anybody's hat, Two anybody except One's, and so on. 3. Once learnt, let players weave about trying not to lose hats. 4. Hats MUST be thrown at the feet, never kicked or thrown away, which breaks the pattern. 5. Play scenes while continuing the insanity: burgling a house with people asleep upstairs, packing for holidays, meeting another pecking order also taking hats.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** Spectators collapse laughing. You cannot teach it unless you are yourself high and expressing great drive. Keep them high on the game and they use their bodies like excellent physical comedians, with no trace of self-consciousness.

## 32. Status Tower
**Players:** 2 or more entering in sequence
**Setup:** Bare stage
**Goal:** Naturalistic pecking-order building.
**Rules:** 1. Someone begins with some low-status activity. 2. Each person entering plays a step higher. 3. Alternatively start at the top and add each person one step down.
**Variations:** None given
**Coaching:** More naturalistic than the clown versions, good bridge to scripted work.

## 33. Maximum Gap: Finger-Snap Deaths
**Players:** Master plus successive servants, coach
**Setup:** Stage cleared for bodies. Master plays as high as possible, servants as low as possible.
**Goal:** Explore extreme status gaps and teach servants attention to the master.
**Rules:** 1. The moment the master feels the slightest irritation he snaps his fingers: the servant commits suicide and a replacement enters. 2. Prod reluctant masters: the coach calls Kill him! until masters kill on their own impulse. 3. Ask the master to explain each death, stressing he need not be fair: waved arms, clumping, disrespect, misunderstanding requirements. 4. Then give each servant THREE lives, dying at the third snap. Watch them do exactly the same thing after a finger snap as before: Do something different, he's about to kill you again! 5. Once established, send in a third person who must placate the master and cope with the servants.
**Variations:** Direct precursor of the King Game in Impro for Storytellers, which formalises timing and competition.
**Coaching:** Servants survive seconds at first, minutes soon, and masters begin to feel pampered. Servants usually think the master harsh; audiences are amazed they survive so long.

## 34. Reversed-Expectation Status Scenes
**Players:** 2
**Setup:** Roles implying fixed status, played inverted
**Goal:** Easy comic scenes from status-role mismatch.
**Rules:** 1. Executioner playing as LOW as possible: too nervous to roll the last cigarette, apologises for the untidiness, asks the condemned for an autograph, accidentally shoots himself in the foot. 2. Ledge suicide playing HIGH: argues the rescuer into jumping off. 3. Bank scene: low-status robber, high-status cashier. Excuse me, Miss... Next cashier please, I'm going off duty... I've got a note. Here. Hand over the money? This is a stick-up! Not so loud... I shall have to refer this to Mr Carbuncle.
**Variations:** Any role pair with a built-in hierarchy.
**Coaching:** Very easy to create scenes this way. Maximum-gap transactions are the basis of absurd drama.
