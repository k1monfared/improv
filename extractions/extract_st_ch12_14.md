CHANGING THE BODY IMAGE
Players: Solo or whole group
Setup: Open training space, no equipment unless a trigger needs it (masks, makeup, card strips)
Goal: Generate character by altering the body image until the self transforms
Rules:
1. Pick a physical trigger and sustain it while moving and speaking. Triggers from the text:
2. Mouth as small as possible while speaking loudly (becomes a tyrant). Top teeth exposed, biting bottom lip, head pushed forward with a giggle (submissive creature).
3. Both rows of teeth bared, breath hissing audibly, eyes wide, aware of peripheral space.
4. Michael Chekhov: place the mind in a body part or outside it, e.g. a silver ball in the chest radiating light (Siegfried hero), or a small hard black steely ball in one eye, then move while sustaining the sensation.
5. Viola Spolin: adjectives on one colour of paper strips, body parts on another. Pair arbitrarily: happy feet, silly bottom, proud chest, nasty nose. Try two at a time (crazy fingers with lustful lips, embarrassed knees with sensual mouth). Play families sharing angry chins and slow thighs while onlookers guess the qualities.
6. Wear a mask sustaining whatever sound it suggests. White face paint and meet the stranger in the mirror. Makeup, wigs, fake moustaches, false cheeks.
7. Turn inarticulate sounds into a voice that is not yours. Ape your pet. Imitate your parents. Go disguised into the real world and play scenes in public.
Variations: Whole families share the same quality, guessing games for the audience, public disguise outings
Coaching: All body-image work fixes attention on the present. Disguised play must leave people in a better state than you found them, and avoid discovery or arrest.

MACHINES
Players: 4 or more, ideally whole team
Setup: Empty stage
Goal: Group rhythm and desensitization for shy players. Johnstone rates it useless as spectacle
Rules:
1. Player 1 stands and repeats one gesture and one sound in a mechanical loop.
2. Player 2 joins alongside with a different gesture and sound, synchronized into the whole.
3. Further players add on until a complete machine wheezes, hisses and chugs.
Variations: Build the machine for a "new employee" to operate, Chaplin-style ingestion included. Assemble an oracle machine that answers a question one word at a time
Coaching: Not recommended for public use. Building a machine merely as an effect is cowardly because there is no risk, and as a challenge both teams built similar machines, doubly tedious

PEOPLE MACHINES
Players: Half dozen volunteers plus one subject
Setup: Class that already knows the Machine Game and knows each other well
Goal: Sharpen observation of real people, encourage mimicry patience, bond the group
Rules:
1. Construct a "Keith" machine: each component performs one characteristic gesture and sound of the subject.
2. Components enter one at a time, never all together.
3. Move on to machines based on other teachers or celebrities. Study celebrities on videotape.
4. Only then invite students to volunteer themselves as subjects.
Variations: The finished machine spreads the subject across the stage like a cubist painting
Coaching: Never let players volunteer each other. One imitator gives offence, a whole group doing it does not. Almost nobody sustains imitation study alone, this game supplies the encouragement

BEING ANIMALS
Players: Entire group
Setup: Open space
Goal: Dissolve shyness and bodily-contact taboos, then build covert animal-spirit characters
Rules:
Stage One: 1. Whole group becomes the SAME animal. Give parties for identical animals: mice squeak, groom whiskers, nibble cheese. Pigs grunt, roll in mud, fight for swill. Snakes hiss with sibilants but walk upright, slitheringly.
2. Answer to "how much human?": be so like the animal we would lock you up. Wolves wolf their food, kittens romp, lizards stare and flick tongues. Contact taboos drop, snakes slide over each other, cats rub.
Stage Two (covert animals): 3. Be human beings with the spirit of an animal, avoiding anything that would get a human classified as insane.
4. Run mixed parties (snakes with mice, dogs with cats) where everyone is on best behaviour and obliged to be courteous.
5. Class votes for the best snake, bear, etc. Then play scenes casting the winners: the winning bear is a teacher interviewing a slithery parent, or a chicken answers a "companionship" advert and arrives at Mr Wolf's home.
Variations: Feeds directly into Mouse Police
Coaching: Physical transformation games fix attention on the body, bodies only exist in the present, so partners rapport well

MOUSE POLICE
Players: 2 or more police (humans), 1 or more concealed animals, supporting household cast
Setup: Domestic scene hiding covert animals (graduates of Being Animals Stage Two)
Goal: Accelerate the covert conversion, and as an entertainment the animal MUST be caught
Rules:
1. Animal Police (who are not that animal) arrive on a report that such animals have moved into the area.
2. They question, hunt clues, notice suspicious behaviour (excessive sibilants, not blinking, mouse patties in the fridge, a hole instead of basement stairs).
3. Householders stay covert, denying everything while leaking traits.
4. Cover blows, the caught animal goes completely animal (hysterical wriggling under furniture, tail around piano legs), police pin and haul away.
5. Afterbeat: householders lament the lodger they hardly knew.
Variations: Any animal police pairing, any household
Coaching: Great pleasure lies in watching total commitment the instant the cover blows. Without a catch the audience gets no pay-off

OBSESSIONS
Players: 2 or more (one obsessed player plus straight partners)
Setup: Ordinary realistic scene, classic version is a homecoming
Goal: Play any feeling as an obsession by basing it on a sustained sound and backward attention
Rules:
1. Choose the obsession: Instant Death (default teaching version), horror of physical contact, insane craving for contact, or any feeling you can find a sound for.
2. Sustain the emotional sounds the WHOLE scene: gasps, pants, screams, or disgusted yuks, or erotic moans. The sounds jerk the body about, the body must never be still while making them. Intersperse dialogue between sounds.
3. Attention leaps constantly and especially to the space BEHIND: worst terrors or delights lurk just outside peripheral vision. Each leap finds fresh hallucination: razor rising through floorboards, poison gas in the chair arm, arsenic wallpaper, pigeon from the bacterial warfare lab, vase that moved six inches.
4. Believe death is seconds away, never plan ahead. Thinking instead of reading kills the game.
5. Partner resists as a normal person (boyfriend told she has never been like this and must calm her down).
6. Grade responses: a dog-phobic alarms at leash, kennel, drawing or toy dog, but far less than a drooling Rottweiler.
Variations: Psychotic version for audience delight (their secret feelings personified), normal version stays just this side of insanity and is more useful to actors. Cross-casting: teacher with touch horror meets student with contact lust, give the student a resistance (fear of expulsion, awe)
Coaching: The secret is the gasp. Tell players to make the sound only AFTER they fail at pure imagination, so they understand its value

FAST-FOOD LABAN
Players: 4 or 5 per group scene, later 2 per scene
Setup: Pin up the wall chart: WEIGHT (heavy/light), SPACE (direct/indirect), TIME (sudden/sustained). One pick per column gives eight effort actions
Goal: Transform players through a sustained movement quality legible to an audience
Rules:
1. The eight combinations: Heavy-Direct-Sudden punching, Heavy-Direct-Sustained pushing, Heavy-Indirect-Sudden slashing, Heavy-Indirect-Sustained wringing, Light-Indirect-Sustained stroking, Light-Indirect-Sudden flitting, Light-Direct-Sudden dabbing, Light-Direct-Sustained smoothing. (Chart lives in the chapter text, Johnstone's cut-down of Laban's twenty-four items.)
2. Four or five players agree on one action. "You are the Punch family, everything you do or say has to be punchy. Decide relationships quickly, show us the Punch family coming down to breakfast." Father rips the newspaper opening it, Mum slams dishes down.
3. Once skilled, play scenes in twos with OPPOSING qualities: punchy landlord with stroking tenant, dabbing doctor with wringing patient.
4. Replay with types switched: slashing teacher with smoothing pupil becomes slashing pupil with smoothing teacher.
5. Choose qualities privately, audience guesses. Interrupt, forbid even nodding, collect guesses, resume and repeat until guesses are right.
Variations: Teach together with Fast-Food Stanislavsky. Combine with status work and lists
Coaching: Qualities describe body use, not actual bodies: Fatty Arbuckle and Oliver Hardy were heavy men, light performers. Accuracy of guessing matters less than transformation. A wrong guess tells you what to eliminate. Naturally light players must practise heavy and vice versa, an improviser should assume any way of moving

FAST-FOOD STANISLAVSKY (PURPOSE LISTS)
Players: Groups write, 2 to 4 play a scene
Setup: Tear paper into long strips, write a purpose atop each (father: to give people a good time, mother: to get sympathy from other people, son: to give people a bad time, daughter-in-law: to be thought intelligent). Divide class into husbands, wives, sons, daughters, each writes about six actions serving its purpose. Model lists live in the chapter text (Good Time, Bad Time, Thought Intelligent, Thought a Hero, Life and Soul of the Party, Thought Normal, To Seduce, Thought Beautiful, Thought Tidy, Not-to-Commit-a-Murder), and Appendix One holds a printed selection of Fast-Food Stanislavsky Lists
Goal: Give permission to create alien characters, stop forward thinking, make purpose produce action
Rules:
1. Trap first: play a death-bed scene (Father dying, Mother nursing, Son arriving with Wife). It comes out solemn and hollow because players try to SHOW sadness.
2. Assign purposes. Knowing purpose alone still bores, the HOW matters.
3. Hand each player their list held like a script. Items are in no order: choose ANY item you can make truthful. Your own ideas are welcome, good ones get added to the list afterwards.
4. Truth rule: a purpose needs resistance and the best resistance is the truth. Do not stride in and tickle the dying man.
5. Coach shouts "Something else from the list!" every five to fifteen seconds to force transitions, beginners cling to item one.
6. Salt safe lists with bold items: hit, tickle, find a zit and panic, tie to bed.
7. Extend: groups write more lists (To be Thought a Saint, To Ruin Someone's Life, To be Stan Laurel, Mae West, Gorilla, Mad Scientist, WASP, John Wayne, To Relate Well to the Audience). Virginia Satir's disturbed-family roles adapt as lists: To be a Computer, To be a Confuser, To Accept Guilt, To Make Guilty.
Variations: Party warm-up: a list per guest, spare pile at the side so players swap lists and rejoin as someone new. Family scenes where ALL relatives play the same list, weirdness feels secure because shared. Timid players take Bad Time or Life-of-the-Party lists, anxiety drops because it was on the list
Coaching: Lists are another device against thinking ahead. They license rough-and-tumble, mock insults, tickling, self-abuse and confession. They work with status transactions and Fast-Food Laban. When players are truthful it looks like genius direction. Problems: scanners hunting the best item get six-item lists, laugh-getters choosing incongruous items get told to accept only what they can make truthful, one-item stayers have outsiders shout their list at them

HAVING A PURPOSE IS NOT ENOUGH (SHOUTED TACTICS DRILL)
Players: 1 player plus director, optional second player
Setup: Simple aim scene, e.g. character aims to get money
Goal: Prove that purpose without tactics is inert, and hand the player massive permission
Rules:
1. Player states aim, plays politely toward it, stalls on bridging behaviour.
2. Director restarts the scene and shouts tactics continuously: Offer your labour! Steal something! Steal something else! Try blackmail! Offer violence! Beg! Weep! Flatter him! Offer to prostitute yourself!
3. Scene fills with incident and the player suddenly seems talented, because the director owns any transgression.
Variations: None needed
Coaching: Permission is the mechanism, the shouted instruction absorbs blame

TO-SEDUCE LISTS
Players: Separate men's and women's writing groups, then 1 champion per side plus scene partner
Setup: Huddles at opposite sides of the room each write a To-Seduce-a-Person-of-the-Opposite-Sex list. Model lists in chapter text (smile, be vulnerable, move sensually, candlelit dinner versus make her laugh, be athletic, be rich, lock door, offer money)
Goal: Strong comic situations from joky instructions
Rules:
1. Each group chooses its own champion to play the list at a partner.
2. Establish STRONG resistance or you will need buckets of water: the woman must not be thought a whore, the man fears putting her off.
3. Remember the real-world case: the person you seduce may be programmed to give you a bad time.
Variations: Feed into Using Combined Lists (seducing one person while giving another a bad time)
Coaching: Expect explosions of laughter during writing. Even joky items like lock door and offer money create strong situations

ANY PURPOSE FOR ANY SCENE
Players: 1 or more per scene
Setup: Any scene, improvised or scripted (running example: a student murders her professor, Danish fragment "Come in, Nielsen")
Goal: Break the belief that analysis of the text yields the purpose, replace cliché playing with character
Rules:
1. Separate text from purpose: murdering the professor is the text, what happens. The purpose could be anything, even To Be Thought Clean and Tidy.
2. Take a random list and pursue its items INSIDE the required events. The tidy murderer dabs a blood spot from her dress, closes the corpse's eyes, polishes its spectacles.
3. Absurd-seeming pairings work: a Good-Time list on a murder means cheerful greeting through the door, coffee for the boss, endowing him with humour, sweet-in-mouth gun trick, cushion placed under his falling head.
4. Negative purposes work: write a Not-To-Commit-a-Murder list (enter reluctantly, show him the gun, give him a chance to escape). The actor speaks as if words are torn out, sweeps the desk then apologizes, weeps, hands back the gun, wrestles it back because the scenario forces the shot.
5. Written text: apply a list but respect the text, keep only usable items. Of the To-be-Thought-Beautiful list the first eight items fit nearly any text, others adapt (worry about flab via mirror business, exercise on a rowing machine mid-quarrel) and some must drop (name dropping, being interviewed). Demonstration: the Nielsen interview fragment replayed with random list pairs, then again with fresh pairs, each version transforms both actors.
Variations: Random draw makes the point fastest
Coaching: There is no best purpose and none could be found by analysing text. Actors have rolled on the floor with elation discovering this

USING COMBINED LISTS
Players: 3, each playing TWO lists aimed at different partners
Setup: Proficient students. Sample assignment: Liz gives boyfriend Brian a bad time AND plays a To-Seduce list at Charlie. Brian gives Liz a good time AND plays To-be-Thought-Intelligent at Charlie. Charlie convinces Liz he is normal AND milks sympathy from Brian
Goal: Layered behaviour like real social life
Rules:
1. Each player runs two simultaneous lists, addressing different behaviours to different partners in the same scene.
2. Advanced form: start on one list, then gradually dissolve into the second list across the scene.
Variations: Any list pairing that can coexist, not all pairs work
Coaching: We already do the dissolve effortlessly whenever meeting strangers and revising our opinion of them

LISTS AS PERFORMANCE GAMES
Players: 2 or more, optionally an audience volunteer
Setup: Prepared lists, Johnstone notes lists are not really suited to public performance but offers two versions
Goal: Let an audience enjoy list-driven play
Rules:
1. Version one: announce each instruction loudly in a detached way BEFORE obeying it. The audience enjoys seeing you controlled by an outside force, and announced items like invade his space or kill something create anticipation.
2. Version two: enlist audience volunteers to read the instructions out, asking them to steer toward some sort of story.
Variations: Combine both
Coaching: Anticipation is the engine, so announce clearly

BELL AND BUZZER
Players: 1 volunteer controller plus 2 to 4 scene players cast as the volunteer's family
Setup: Volunteer holds a bell and a buzzer. Game reached the Loose Moose from Orlando, excellent addition to the Life Game
Goal: Shape the scene toward the volunteer's felt truth, strip out pointless blocking
Rules:
1. Volunteer casts the players as his own family.
2. Players improvise. BELL rings when the playing is on the right track, BUZZER sounds when a guess is wrong.
3. On a buzzer, players revise their course and try again.
Variations: Abuse version: groups buzz arbitrary choices just to thwart actors for laughs (I'll get in the car, BZZZT, I won't get in the car). Preferred version reverses it to punish blocking: I won't get in the car, BZZZT, I'll get in the car, RING
Coaching: Prefer the anti-blocking use over the thwarting use

SCENES WITH FINGERS
Players: 3 or more operators, each operating one hand of finger characters
Setup: Small theatre with good sight lines, empty floor as alien planet. Invented by Jim Curry via a Frisbee landing
Goal: Total absorption (operators are undefended instantly) and a spectacle unlike anything else
Rules:
1. Walk middle and index fingers about the stage as astronaut explorers, voicing squeaky dialogue for them.
2. Creatures interact: scanner detects alien life form, a gloved-hand monster scrabbles in, phasers on kill, monster leaps on a finger, is shot, flops onto its back motionless.
3. Scale jumps allowed: a third operator walks a nude Barbie doll as a grieving giant whose "poor Glorp" was killed. Both teams creep on, each pair of hands a creature. A gigantic real hand thrusts through curtains and all creatures kneel as if God entered.
Variations: Potion turns a player into a giant, companions flee on fingertips in squeaky voices as the slow-motion giant pursues
Coaching: Nobody is ever self-conscious about their fingers' acting, so absorption is instant. Do not hide the hands. Finger characters are happy, perky, tiny voiced. Suits only smallish theatres

PEOPLE AS OBJECTS
Players: Scene players plus any number of object players
Setup: Traditional mime game, no props
Goal: Generosity mood-setter and living scenery
Rules:
1. Improvisers become whatever the scene needs: chairs, tables, telephones, a bench to sit on, polar-bear rug, shower.
2. Commit fully and hold: waxworks, statues, mirrors, sofas, footstools, slide-show projections, horses giving piggy-back rides. One player spent an ENTIRE scene as an armchair and caused a sensation.
Variations: Challenge phrasing: "We challenge you to the best use of the other team!"
Coaching: Too repetitive as a standalone challenge. Always available to spice up other games. No one worries whether their mime is good, offering yourself puts everyone in a good mood

DENNIS'S PUPPET SHOW
Players: 2 audience volunteers as puppeteers, 1 backstage helper, 1 jaded-showman host
Setup: Puppet theatre of three folding sections with an oblong hole (improvise one over the back of a sofa). Puppeteers MUST be hidden, that is where the fun is. Backstage helper hands puppets, whispers advice, holds the microphone. Created by Dennis Cahill of Loose Moose
Goal: Audience delight and rapid de-inhibition of volunteers
Rules:
1. Host invents a story for glove puppets operated live by the two volunteers.
2. Host plays a showman so jaded and disillusioned that inept, frightened puppets do not matter to the fiction.
3. Backstage feeds puppets and whispered advice throughout.
4. Volunteers typically shed inhibition fast, and spectators reward them eagerly.
Variations: Any venue with a concealment line
Coaching: Hide the operators so the audience imagines their difficulties

CHANGING THE OBJECT
Players: Circle of any size
Setup: Sit in a circle, one mimed object travels the circle (French mime exercise)
Goal: Relaxation, dropping intellectual control, spontaneity under observation
Rules:
1. Receive the mimed object, use it as offered (lick the ice cream), then CHANGE it into something else before passing (envelope).
2. Keep a blank mind, let ideas float in one ear and out the other. Pre-planning fails: intend mouse, receive mouse.
3. Blank on receipt? HANDLE it: roll gum into a ball, pull pieces, stretch, rotate, weigh, stroke, observe disinterestedly. Stretching may become peeling off a surgical glove. Never force.
4. If it will not alter, pass it on "as is". Johnstone defines the non-changers as the only ones provably honest, reframing failure as success.
5. Bad mime? Announce what it became, humility reads well.
6. Openly chart the anxiety curve: dread peaks as the object approaches, collapses once it passes. Shared fear dissolves in discussion. Note that mime-trained students suffer MOST, tempted toward old material.
Variations: CHANGING REAL OBJECTS (Tom Osborn): walking stick becomes umbrella, harpoon or ear cleaner. Shoe pin becomes grenade. Chair hugged to the chest becomes bagpipes. Keep a heap of props stage side: bench becomes canoe, sofa becomes pool, boat up-ended becomes shrine. Audience donates props
Coaching: Handle things instead of deciding, they change of their own accord. Your imperfect mime alters objects anyway: the alarm clock shrinks as you wind it, the yo-yo resembles a bouncing ball. Detach ego from success and failure and ideas flood in: fishing rod to lasso, barbell to trapeze

STEALING
Players: 2 (authority figure plus thief), optional offstage responder as Nurse
Setup: Table piled high with junk, NOTHING breakable. Thief wears a huge coat with extra pockets sewn into the lining, or an over-large boiler suit. Optional man-mask or fright wig with false nose. Spin-off of the Making Faces Game, a staple for touring to audiences with no English
Goal: Visual lazzi, steal the set unseen
Rules:
1. Authority runs a consultation (Doctor receiving patient Pat).
2. Doctor looks away on plausible pretexts: fetching the file, turning to consult a book, bending to search the desk.
3. Each look-away lets Pat palm items into the clothing: doll, hot-water bottle, tubing, kettle examined innocently then returned minus the tubing, pen and whistle palmed from under the Doctor's nose.
4. Anything visibly bulging MUST be protested and retrieved by the Doctor while Pat apologizes sincerely and steals yet more, picking the Doctor's pockets.
5. Escalate: boiler suit fattens into a fat suit, umbrella juts from a trouser leg, flag pokes from the collar, stolen goods start re-emerging and get shoved back.
6. Set-piece endings: the Doctor shakes Pat upside down while dozens of objects clatter down, or Pat crams the DOCTOR into the boiler suit (John Breem's ending).
Variations: Balloon pop as punctuation, Nurse called to search for the missing mutator
Coaching: Non-breakable props only. The pleasure is sincere apology combined with continued theft

THE KNIFE GAME
Players: 2 ideally (couple frame), 1 workable
Setup: Mimed knife and knife box. Works as symbol for the concealed self, for sex, or for becoming aliens in sci-fi scenes
Goal: Shocking, eerie skinning transformation
Rules:
1. Handle the mimed knife as if very sharp, perhaps throw it at a wall, then grip it two-handed, blade upward, feigning mental stress.
2. Delay, ram the blade deep into the forehead, scream terrible agony, recover.
3. Seize the hilt and drag the blade down face and torso, kept at right angles to the flesh (careful at the groin), on down one leg, screaming or groaning. Lay the knife on an imaginary surface, breathe easier.
4. Press fingertips into the top of the slit and RIP the edges apart with birthy gasping, groaning, crying, howling. Such sounds release emotion in onlookers.
5. Ease the new distorted face out feature by grotesque feature. Use the freed arm to drag the other from its sleeve, then use that deformed arm to free its twin. Roll the skin down the body with excited, pleasurable sounds, peel it off like tights, hang it on a hook or fold it.
6. Adore the new body: stroke it, gloat in a mirror, leap about with enormous energy.
Performance frames: babysitter admits boyfriend with "Did you bring the knives?", child wakes, parents return early, mix-up swapping skins back on. Motel honeymoon: heirloom chrome-steel knives, "Do me first, Peter," forehead stab, skin removed, newly skinned player becomes reptilian, hisses, throws the partner down and slits HIM open.
Variants: Room-mate discovered to be an alien. Lover revealed as a snake person mid-slough. Refuser gets skinned forcibly. Lizards smoke a cigarette afterward, room service arrives and gets skinned too.
Coaching: Seldom used, SAVE IT FOR SPECIAL OCCASIONS. Done well it is astounding violence, psychotic and intensely sexual. Push sounds, "More sounds! Be more reptilian!"

HAND ON KNEE
Players: 3 (offender in the middle, one victim each side)
Setup: Three plain chairs with touching edges, pretending to be on a train. Discovered by Roddy Maude-Roxby of the Theatre Machine
Goal: Moment-by-moment realism: the struggle to behave normally becomes memorable as well as funny
Rules:
1. Middle player speaks to one neighbour while placing a hand on the OTHER neighbour's knee, with no awareness of doing it.
2. MANDATORY DRILL first: run the crossed action (talk left, touch right, talk right, touch left) fifteen to twenty times, faster each round. Without it the player touches whoever he addresses or touches nobody.
3. Brief the victims: start low-key, build to paroxysms of rage and embarrassment. The offender never notices, so he experiences himself as the tormented one.
4. Establish the train slowly FIRST: adverts, window, newspaper, mild shyness, let spectators wonder why this tableau is shown.
5. Deliver short remarks across the offending hand, then stare straight ahead so the problem evaporates before the victim decides what to do. Plonk the hand down FIRMLY. Let the victims simmer.
6. Victims play normal people suppressing scandal: inner questions (is he mad, or eccentric, did the others notice), a single gulp, skirt tugged half an inch, an "Excuse me?" answered by "Yes?" that collapses into "Oh, nothing." Do NOT cross legs away, pretend a wall stands beside you. Everyone jogs with the train's motion.
7. Coach escalates: squeeze her knee, make her jump. Squeeze his harder, force him to act. Mid-scene, order the offender NEUTRAL again and study three ruffled people in silence, sustainable for a long beat.
8. If a victim lifts the hand and parks it on the offender's own knee, rewind to the touch: a stranger would certainly notice. Escalate through grabbed wrists, purse beating, standing threats to throw him off the train, then a frozen fuming tableau.
Endings NOT built in (options only): ticket collector arrives, genuine horrified realization of guilt, seat-swap rescue where the rescuer clasps HER knee as lights fade.
Variations: None recorded beyond ending choices
Coaching: Played purely for laughs it is filler and earns no sympathy. Attended moment by moment it lands. Once a year maximum

SPASMS
Players: 2 minimum plus director with clap
Setup: Open stage, entrances from opposite sides. Seen as a front-curtain set-change cover at a pantomime, presumed ancient lazzi
Goal: Pay-off in the partner's escalating reactions, ending in shared contagion
Rules:
1. Two strangers cross, double-take, recognize each other, greet effusively, swap information.
2. One player spasms at IRREGULAR intervals with a loud shriek and has NO awareness of doing it.
3. He continues baffled by her reaction, accuses HER of acting strangely. She demonstrates the spasm to explain, and catches it herself.
4. Both now spasm, growing angrier or happier, exiting one pursued by the other through gales of laughter.
Technique: the sound must be ELATED, the spasm OPENS the body rather than contracting it, conveying a flash of manic happiness. Grotesque versions excite horror instead. Identical form every single time, beginners let theirs shrink as confidence drains. Contest option: line players up, hand over each head in turn, crowd chooses by applause.
Timing drill: trainees may spasm ONLY when the director claps, and the director claps at the worst possible moments (mid-burglary whisper) and pointedly WITHHOLDS claps when both brace for one.
Variant: the spasmer starts believing his partner's accusations. Almost never seen, must be forced, improvisers hate losing control
Coaching: Regularity kills it. Irregularity plus identical form is the whole trick

PAPER-FLICKING
Players: 2 (agent plus destitute applicant)
Setup: Desk, notepad, plentiful paper, reserve balls pre-screwed and hidden under the desk papers. Inspired by the Spasm Game
Goal: Comic gap between spoken approval and physical abuse
Rules:
1. High-status agent interviews the desperate applicant, promising fame and fortune, oozing approval, writing down his details.
2. The INSTANT each page is written, rip it out, screw it into a tight little ball, hurl it violently at the applicant's face. Intervals unpredictable.
3. First hit draws rage, interrupt: You need this job! You can't pay the rent! Children to feed! Maybe it's a test!
4. Resume. Hits get ignored, then interactions bloom: Ow! I'm sorry? You threw paper at me! Impossible! The applicant unwraps a ball and gently pushes his phone number back across the desk, weeping, Please take it!
5. Agent alternates insane violence WHILE throwing with total approval BETWEEN throws, and believes himself kind and generous throughout.
6. Screw balls tight, throw so they STING, with a cry of violent rage. Audience wants many balls, so preload.
Variant: suitor scene, a woman totally receptive while clubbing him at unpredictable intervals. Victim may attempt joining the joke, hurling a ball back, and gets ruled mentally unstable for an explosives factory.
Coaching: The victim must be genuinely desperate for the job. Unpredictability of the throw is everything
